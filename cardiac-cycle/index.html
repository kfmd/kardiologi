# Phase Part of Cycle Pericardial Rub? Rub Mechanism Heart Murmurs & Auscultatory Findings
Late Diastole
1
Atrial Systole
(Atrial Kick)
→ Presystolic rub component · S4 territory
 Late Diastole Yes Atrial contraction moves blood forcefully into ventricle → cardiac displacement within pericardial sac. Softest component; absent in AF.
Presystolic Murmur
Low-pitched rumble crescendos just before S1. Caused by atrial systole forcing blood through a narrowed mitral valve. Best heard: apex, patient in left lateral decubitus.
Presystolic Murmur
Similar presystolic rumble at left lower sternal border. Increases with inspiration (Carvallo's sign).
S4 Gallop
S4 occurs as atrial kick strikes a stiff, non-compliant ventricle. Indicates diastolic dysfunction. Loud S4 is classic in HCM.
Systole
2
Isovolumetric Contraction
→ S1 closes · No volume change
 Early Systole No All valves closed. Pressure builds internally — no net cardiac displacement to generate friction.
Ejection Click
Early systolic click occurs as the domed/bicuspid valve leaflets abruptly halt at maximum opening displacement. Heard at onset of ejection just after S1.
Mitral Click
Mid-systolic click (can begin here) as the mitral leaflets prolapse into the LA. Position changes timing: standing moves click earlier; squatting moves it later.
Loud S1
Thickened but mobile mitral leaflets snap shut with increased force, producing a loud (sometimes palpable) S1.
3
Rapid Ventricular Ejection
→ Systolic rub (loudest) · Peak aortic pressure
 Mid Systole Yes Greatest cardiac motion of the entire cycle. Forceful ejection maximally shifts the heart in the pericardial sac. Always loudest, most consistent rub component.
Systolic Ejection Murmur
Harsh, crescendo-decrescendo (diamond-shaped) murmur at right upper sternal border (RUSB). Radiates to carotids. Later peak = more severe. Associated with slow-rising carotid pulse (pulsus parvus et tardus) and absent A2.
Systolic Ejection Murmur
Dynamic LVOT obstruction → harsh crescendo-decrescendo murmur at LLSB/apex. Unique feature: murmur increases with Valsalva, standing, amyl nitrite (decreased preload) and decreases with squatting, leg raise (increased preload). No radiation to carotids.
Systolic Ejection Murmur
Crescendo-decrescendo murmur at left upper sternal border (LUSB). Increases with inspiration. Associated with wide split S2 and RV heave.
Pansystolic Murmur
Blowing, high-pitched holosystolic murmur at apex, radiating to axilla. Constant throughout systole (same LV-LA pressure gradient). Acute MR (papillary muscle rupture): decrescendo, ends before S2 as pressures equalize quickly.
Pansystolic Murmur
Blowing holosystolic murmur at LLSB. Increases dramatically with inspiration (Carvallo's sign distinguishes from MR). Associated with pulsatile liver, prominent V wave in JVP.
Pansystolic Murmur
Harsh holosystolic murmur at 3rd–4th LLSB with thrill. Loudness inversely relates to defect size (small = louder).
Late Systolic Murmur
Late systolic murmur follows the mid-systolic click as prolapsed leaflets permit regurgitation. May become holosystolic with severe MR. Maneuvers that decrease LV volume move click and murmur earlier.
Innocent / Flow Murmur
Grade I–II vibratory or musical ejection murmur, heard in children or high-output states (anemia, fever, pregnancy, hyperthyroidism). Disappears with Valsalva.
4
Reduced Ventricular Ejection
→ Declining aortic pressure phase
 Late Systole No Low-force, decelerating ejection. Insufficient cardiac motion to generate audible friction.
Decrescendo Murmur
The decrescendo limb of the HCM systolic murmur falls through this phase as LVOT gradient decreases with late systolic narrowing of the ventricle.
Dicrotic Notch
The dicrotic notch (incisura) represents aortic valve closure. Absent/blunted in severe AR (wide pulse pressure). Prominent in vasodilated states. Bisferiens pulse (double-peaked) in combined AS+AR or HCM.
Diastole
5
Isovolumetric Relaxation
→ S2 closes · All valves closed
 Early Diastole No All valves closed. Pressure releases internally — mirrors Phase 2. No net cardiac displacement.
S2 Splitting
Normal: physiological split widens on inspiration. Wide fixed split: ASD (equalized pressures) or RBBB (delayed P2). Paradoxical split: LBBB or severe AS (delayed A2, split narrows on inspiration). Loud A2: systemic HTN. Soft A2: severe AS. Loud P2: pulmonary hypertension.
Pericardial Knock
High-pitched early diastolic sound (louder than S3) at apex/LLSB due to abrupt cessation of ventricular filling by a rigid pericardium. Occurs right after S2, earlier than S3.
6
Rapid Ventricular Filling
→ Protodiastolic rub component · S3 territory
 Early Diastole Yes Rapid passive filling shifts cardiac position — second largest cardiac motion of the cycle.
Early Diastolic Murmur
High-pitched, blowing, decrescendo murmur starting immediately after A2. Best heard: left sternal border (3rd ICS), patient leaning forward, breath held in expiration. Austin Flint murmur: mid-diastolic rumble at apex (AR jet strikes MV). Associated signs: wide pulse pressure, Corrigan's pulse, Quincke's sign, Duroziez's sign, de Musset's sign.
Early Diastolic Murmur
Graham Steell murmur: high-pitched decrescendo at LUSB, increases with inspiration. Usually in pulmonary hypertension. Rare in isolated PR.
Opening Snap
High-pitched sound as the thickened-but-pliable mitral valve is snapped open by elevated LA pressure. Earlier after S2 = more severe MS (higher LA-LV gradient). Absent when valve is heavily calcified.
Mid-Diastolic Rumble
Low-pitched rumble following the opening snap. Heard at apex with bell of stethoscope, patient in left lateral decubitus. Grade correlates with severity in sinus rhythm.
S3 Gallop
S3 occurs as rapid filling abruptly decelerates against a dilated, volume-overloaded or non-compliant ventricle. Pathological in adults >40 years. Normal in children and young athletes.
7
Reduced Filling
(Diastasis)
→ Slow equilibration phase
 Mid Diastole No Slow, low-pressure filling. Minimal cardiac motion; insufficient to overcome friction threshold.
Mid-Diastolic Rumble
The rumble of MS can extend into mid-diastole, becoming quieter during diastasis (reduced flow), then intensifying again in Phase 1 as atrial systole creates a presystolic accentuation.
Austin Flint Murmur
Mid-diastolic rumble at apex mimicking MS. AR jet causes functional stenosis of the mitral valve by displacing the anterior MV leaflet. No opening snap. Differentiated with echocardiography.
Continuous Murmur
Machinery murmur (continuous systolic-diastolic) heard at LUSB/left infraclavicular area. Peaks at S2 because the Ao-PA gradient is highest when the aortic valve closes. Extends throughout diastasis.
Carey Coombs Murmur
Mid-diastolic murmur of rheumatic mitral valvulitis — due to MV leaflet edema in acute rheumatic fever, not true stenosis.

Rub summary: Triphasic rub = Phases 1+3+6 · Biphasic = 3+6 (absent in AF) · Monophasic = Phase 3 only.  |  Murmur timing rule: Systolic murmurs occur between S1–S2; diastolic murmurs between S2–S1. Continuous murmurs span both. Grading: I–VI (Levine scale).  |  Key maneuvers: Valsalva/standing ↓ preload → ↑ HCM/MVP murmurs; squatting/leg raise ↑ preload → ↓ HCM murmur; inspiration ↑ right-sided murmurs.
